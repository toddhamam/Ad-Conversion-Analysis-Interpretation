# Cold Email Optimization Learnings

## Proven Principles (from cold email research, March 2026)

### Subject Lines
- Plausible deniability beats pain-point subjects. The subject should look like it could be from someone they know
- Lowercase, 2-5 words, no marketing language, no punctuation
- "quick question", "hey {first_name}", "thought of you" — high open rates because they don't reveal intent
- Pain-point subjects ("Ad fatigue?", "creative bottleneck?") get decent opens (82%) but prime the reader to be defensive

### Email Structure (PWOC Framework)
- Personalization (specific observation) → Proof (1 sentence of social proof) → Offer → CTA
- CVR = (ROI x Trust) / Friction. Quantify the ROI, add trust via proof, minimize friction in CTA
- 80% about THEM, 20% about you. Most bad emails are inverted
- Social proof (even brief) dramatically increases trust. Never skip it
- Casualize company names (strip Inc/LLC/Ltd suffixes)

### Follow-ups
- Short bumps only, never re-pitch features in follow-ups
- "Just floating this back up" outperforms feature-heavy follow-ups
- Follow-up 1 at day 2-3, one follow-up max to start

### Copy Rules
- No em dashes (AI giveaway)
- 50-100 words ideal body copy, 150 max
- One CTA only, lowest possible friction
- Write like you'd text a professional acquaintance

### Iteration Strategy
- Big changes early (rounds 1-3): test fundamentally different approaches to eliminate bad categories
- Medium changes middle (rounds 4-6): change 2-3 elements per test
- Small changes late (rounds 7+): isolate single variables to fine-tune

### Hypotheses Worth Testing
- Deliberate typo placed late in email (signals real human at scale)
- Scarcity element ("only onboarding X more brands this quarter")
- Shared identity ("as someone who's also spent too long in Ads Manager...")
- Give-first approach (lead with a free insight before the CTA)
- Direct offer specificity using prospect's ad count data

## What Works
- Two-touch sequence: follow-up 1 boosts replies by 49%
- Under 80 words body copy
- Casual tone, no em dashes, plain text
- Personalization hooks referencing specific company activity
- Mixed subject line pool (pain-point + plausible deniability) for natural A/B testing
