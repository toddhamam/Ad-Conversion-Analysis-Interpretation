## Design Context

### Users
**Primary users**: Founders of SMEs, CMOs, and senior media buyers at growth-stage companies.
**Context**: They open Convertra during work hours, often alongside Ads Manager, analytics dashboards, and Slack. They're time-poor, decision-heavy, and pattern-trained by years of reading dashboards. They need to extract signal from noise fast.
**Job to be done**: Eliminate creative fatigue by automating the ad creative testing flywheel — extract winning patterns from data, generate high-converting creatives, publish, and compound results over time. They want more control over their conversion data and the ability to turn that data into ROAS improvements.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Elite. Enterprise. Efficiency.
**Voice**: Confident and direct — not salesy or hype-driven. Speaks like a senior strategist, not a marketer. Shows competence through clarity and precision, not through decoration or flash.
**Emotional goals**: When a user opens the app or landing page, they should feel:
- **Empowered** — like they have tools and data others don't
- **Efficient** — like complexity has been absorbed, not just presented
- **Clarity** — like they can see clearly and are utilizing data they weren't before

### Aesthetic Direction
**Visual tone**: Warm premium enterprise — the intersection of approachable warmth and institutional credibility. Think Notion's warmth meets Stripe's precision meets Linear's confidence.
**Theme**: Light mode — this is a daytime tool used by professionals in well-lit offices.
**Color palette**: Keep the existing lime (`#d4e157`) primary and violet (`#a855f7`) secondary. These are the brand colors. But use them with more restraint and intention — the 60-30-10 rule. Lime is the 10% accent (CTAs, key actions, success). Violet is a 5% rare accent (premium features, ConversionIQ branding). The remaining 85% should be tinted neutrals, not pure grays.
**Reference**: Arc Ads (arcads.ai) — clean, minimal, intentional, no visual clutter. The quality to channel is their *restraint* and *professionalism*, adapted to our light theme.
**Anti-reference**: Any UI that looks "vibe coded" — the recognizable pattern of AI-generated SaaS products from 2024-2025 characterized by: side-tab accent borders on cards, gradient text on headings, purple-cyan palettes, glassmorphism, generic card grids, identical icon-above-heading layouts, and bounce animations.

### Design Principles

1. **Earned attention** — Every visual element must justify its existence. No decorative borders, no gradient text, no sparklines that don't say anything. If it's there, it's doing work.

2. **Hierarchy through space, not decoration** — Use whitespace, type scale, and weight contrast to create hierarchy. Never use colored border-left stripes, glow effects, or gradient fills to differentiate sections. The layout itself should communicate importance.

3. **Warmth through typography, not through effects** — Use distinctive, carefully paired fonts to create a warm, premium feel. Not through rounded corners, soft shadows, and blur effects — those are the AI shorthand for "friendly." Real warmth comes from considered type choices and generous spacing.

4. **Data clarity over data density** — Dashboards should feel spacious and scannable, not cramped. A CMO glancing at the screen should get the story in 3 seconds. Progressive disclosure for depth — summary first, detail on interaction.

5. **Invisible craft** — The best design is the one nobody notices. No one should open Convertra and think about the UI — they should think about their ad performance. The interface should feel inevitable, not designed.
