# Distribution Strategies

Ranked by effectiveness across the 6 case studies. Each strategy includes who it works for, when to use it, and the specific playbook.

---

## Tier 1: Proven Revenue Drivers

### 1. Affiliate Marketing
**Who used it**: Poppy AI (primary driver), Mike's apps (via AppSumo LTD communities)
**Best for**: B2C and prosumer SaaS with visual/demonstrable value
**Revenue impact**: Poppy went from $500/mo to $150K in 2 weeks from ONE affiliate

**Playbook**:
- Start with 1-2 affiliates to prove the model works
- Pay 50% commission on tracked income (untracked conversions are free revenue)
- Focus on bigger partnerships over mass outreach (Vanessa Lau, Matt Gray-level creators)
- Let affiliates create content in their own style — don't over-prescribe
- Scale to 200+ affiliates once the model is validated
- Unlike product launches (feast/famine), affiliates compound over time

**Key insight**: "You won't believe it works for you until one day it actually does." Most founders dismiss it. Read Alex Hormozi's $100M Leads for the framework.

### 2. Mass Short-Form UGC (Instagram/TikTok)
**Who used it**: Cluely ($6M ARR), referenced by Cali ($30M ARR), Quitter ($500K/mo), WPCam
**Best for**: Consumer and prosumer apps, especially visually demonstrable products
**Revenue impact**: Cluely got 1M+ installs in 2 months from short form alone

**Playbook**:
- Hire 50-60+ content creators (NOT traditional influencers)
- Look for creators with 3K-150K followers who understand content structure
- Pay $25-40/video flat + view bonuses ($500 for 500K views, $1K for 1M views)
- Creators are massively underpaid for their view generation — this is an arbitrage
- "Mandatory scrolling hours" for your team to find new creators
- Manage via group DMs or Discord
- Instagram > TikTok for educational/step-by-step content
- TikTok audience has shorter attention span — different content needed

**Key insight**: "There's a gigantic delta — not enough people are posting content for the amount being consumed." This arbitrage window won't last forever.

### 3. Cold Calling (Professional/Enterprise)
**Who used it**: CrimeAL (only channel that worked)
**Best for**: Professional audiences, high-trust products, enterprise sales
**Revenue impact**: 6 paying customers + 10 trials from cold calling vs. 0 from all other channels combined

**Playbook**:
- Don't sell the software on the call — sell the next step (demo, trial)
- Listen to specific pain points and tailor the pitch live
- You get a firm yes/no much faster than any other channel
- Conference invitations and partnerships emerge naturally from calls
- "Do things that don't scale" early — manual beats automated

**When to use**: When your target customer is in a specific professional niche, values trust, and isn't scrolling social media for solutions.

### 4. AI-Powered SEO Content at Scale
**Who used it**: Arvo case study (600 → 8,500 visits), Mike's apps (content from day 1)
**Best for**: Any SaaS wanting long-term organic traffic + LLM citations
**Revenue impact**: $13K/mo equivalent ad value; 63 ChatGPT citations, 128 Google AI Overview citations

**Playbook**:
- Create custom templates with your proven content framework
- Three article types that work:
  1. **Listicles**: "Top 10 [tools] for [industry]" — high engagement
  2. **Comparisons**: "[Your product] vs [Competitor]" — high purchase intent
  3. **FAQ articles**: "Do I need [X]?" — best for LLM citations (people ask these exact questions to ChatGPT/Gemini)
- Volume matters: 10 articles/day = 300/month
- Start writing content day 1 — "it is never, ever, ever too early"
- Write "alternative to" pages and competitor comparison pages first (Mike's advice)
- Fix technical SEO: schema markup, internal links
- Get high-authority backlinks

**Key insight**: FAQ articles are the most powerful for AI engine citations. Structure content as answers to questions people actually ask LLMs.

## Tier 2: Mind Share & Brand Building

### 5. Controversial/Viral Marketing (X/Twitter)
**Who used it**: Cluely (primary brand strategy)
**Best for**: Founder-led brands, consumer tech, bold founders willing to be polarizing
**Revenue impact**: Not directly attributable to revenue — drives mind share that compounds

**Playbook**:
- Separate marketing into two buckets: "marketing for conversion" (UGC) vs "marketing for mind share" (controversy)
- On X: manufacture controversial scenarios that get people talking
- Create cinematic launch videos modeled after friend.com's $4M launch
- Frame mundane features in controversial ways ("cheat on everything" for an AI assistant)
- Post something launchable every day, not every quarter
- "Mind share = people talking about you at the lunch table. Brand awareness = people have seen you but don't discuss you"

**Key insight**: "You don't need your product to be viral. You need your product to be in a viral situation."

### 6. Lifetime Deals (LTD Pipeline)
**Who used it**: Mike's 5 apps (core playbook step)
**Best for**: New SaaS products needing early capital and users
**Revenue impact**: Mike raised ~$30K from private LTD for Frill; goal is $100K total from LTD cycle

**Playbook**:
1. Private LTD ($59-100) → sell in Reddit, Facebook groups, LTD communities
2. AppSumo LTD → massive reach, huge email database
3. Final private LTD → higher prices, FOMO, close forever
4. Transition to MRR — use LTD capital to fund content and reviews

**Key insight**: LTD customers are your best ambassadors. They write reviews, they give feedback, they want you to succeed. Never give away free accounts — charge from day 1.

## Tier 3: Supporting Channels

### 7. 1-on-1 Onboarding Calls
**Who used it**: Poppy AI (500 calls/month)
**Best for**: Reducing churn, driving referrals, upselling
**Revenue impact**: 20-30% referral rate, reduced refunds and churn

**Key insight**: "Scaling the unscalable" — don't worry about how this scales to 10 billion users. You have a few thousand. Solve for them.

### 8. Reviews & Social Proof
**Who used it**: Mike's apps (step 9 of playbook)
**Best for**: SEO, trust building, conversion optimization

**Key insight**: LTD customers are willing to write reviews. Ask them. Focus on Trust Pilot, G2, and Reddit.

## What DOESN'T Work (Validated Failures)

| Channel | Why It Failed | Source |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| Reddit organic posts | Attracts fans, not buyers. Communities hostile to AI tools | CrimeAL |
| Paid X amplification (Megaphone) | Zero conversions. Scrollers aren't searchers | CrimeAL |
| Cold email alone | Too passive. Easy to ignore. Doesn't build trust | CrimeAL |
| Press/VC outreach | Zero traffic, zero customers. Catch-22 of needing traction to get coverage | CrimeAL |
| Generic influencer sponsorships | Most have fake engagement. Expensive and non-converting | CrimeAL, Cluely |
| AI-generated content (video) | "The human videos are still not exactly human." Algorithm rewards content that feels real | Cluely |

## Channel Selection Framework

| Your Stage | Best Channels |
|------------|---------------|
| Pre-revenue, need first 10 customers | Cold calling (professional), LTDs (prosumer), Affiliates (consumer) |
| $0-$10K MRR, validating PMF | Affiliates, SEO content, LTDs, 1-on-1 onboarding |
| $10K-$100K MRR, scaling | Mass UGC, affiliates at scale, SEO content at volume |
| $100K+ MRR, building moat | Mind share campaigns, sales team, high-ticket upsells |
