# Product-Market Fit Patterns

How 6 AI SaaS founders found (or are finding) product-market fit. Common patterns and contrarian insights.

---

## Pattern 1: Product-Founder Fit > Product-Market Fit

**Source**: Poppy AI (advice from Cliff Weitzman, Speechify founder)

"Forget about product-market fit. Focus on product-founder fit."

- Build what YOU want as a founder, not what the market says
- Poppy's founders used their own product daily for months before anyone bought it
- "We would constantly think, how will we live without this tool?"
- They had zero sales for months but kept building because of personal conviction
- The right market found them through affiliates, not deliberate targeting

**Key quote**: "Conviction comes before clarity. We had conviction and just stuck with it. Clarity is earned as a gift of being convicted in your mission."

---

## Pattern 2: Pick Ideas That Have Been Done Before

**Source**: Mike (5 apps, $200K MRR)

"New ideas are risky. New ideas need validation. If you pick an idea that's been done before, you know that people want it."

- Look for existing tools with bad UX → build a better version
- Validated demand means you can focus on execution, not market education
- Mike specifically avoids AI-focused businesses: "Too many times you have an idea that relies on an API that you do not control"

**Anti-pattern**: Chasing shiny/novel ideas. All of Mike's $200K MRR comes from "boring" businesses (social media aggregator, feedback tool, digital signage, onboarding tours, group ecards).

---

## Pattern 3: Narrow Niche + Specific Pain = No Competition

**Source**: Eugene (micro-SaaS)

"The businesses that make the most money are never the obvious ones. The best niches are narrow, simple, and solve one very specific painful problem."

- If you've heard of the niche, if everyone's talking about it, skip it
- Non-obvious problems have zero competition because nobody thinks to build solutions
- Low price point ($5-20/mo) works because the emotional pain drives demand
- Can be built in hours with AI tools

---

## Pattern 4: Let the Market Tell You Who Your Customer Is

**Source**: Poppy AI

- Originally targeted copywriters → actually found PMF with marketers/founders wanting viral content
- Didn't know their actual market for months
- Discovery came through affiliates: one TikTok hit the right demographic, and suddenly the product clicked
- "Going through a bunch of affiliates, making content, trying all this stuff, eventually one affiliate's TikTok hit the actual demographic"

**Practical implication**: Cast a wide net with affiliates/content, then follow the signal. Don't pre-commit to a single persona.

---

## Pattern 5: Distribution First, Product Second

**Source**: Cluely ($6M ARR)

"We consider ourselves a distribution-first company. If you can get a billion views a month, people will buy."

- Built a tool (InterviewCoder) primarily as a distribution vehicle
- The product evolved (from cheating tool → universal AI assistant) but distribution DNA stayed
- "Right now distribution is more important than product because with AI, things are so easy to build"
- "The only thing that has not been universally distributed is the understanding of what goes viral"

**Counter-example**: CrimeAL founder had deep growth expertise from crypto but couldn't transfer it to a new industry. Distribution knowledge is context-dependent.

---

## Pattern 6: Don't Chase AI as the Core Value Prop

**Source**: Mike (explicitly avoids AI businesses), Eugene (uses AI to build, not as the product)

Mike: "We will never go after an AI focused business. Too many times you have an idea that relies on an API that you do not control."

- Use AI to build faster, but don't make AI the differentiator
- AI APIs can change, pricing can shift, competitors can match features overnight
- Build on proven categories with AI as an accelerant, not the core

**Nuance**: Cluely and Poppy DO have AI as the core product, but:
- Cluely bets on distribution moat, not technical moat: "When the next model comes out, our product gets 4x better"
- Poppy's real moat is the onboarding + community + affiliate flywheel
